EMAIL 2     Day 2     ·     The pre-tax tax bomb
Subject:  Your IRA has a silent partner you never chose
Preview:  Every dollar in there is taxed on the way out.
Look at your IRA or 401(k) balance. Now cross out a quarter to a third of it. That's roughly the government's share — not a fee, not a maybe, but a bill that comes due on your terms or theirs.
Pre-tax retirement accounts are wonderful on the way in and unforgiving on the way out. Every withdrawal is ordinary income. Left unmanaged, that can push you into higher brackets exactly when you'd rather be spending, not filing.
The good news: the timing and order of those withdrawals are yours to design. That design is where a lot of lifetime tax gets won or lost — and it starts years before the first required distribution.
If a large pre-tax balance is most of your net worth, it's worth mapping:  Schedule your Wealth Curve Conversation →

EMAIL 4     Day 6     ·     Sequence-of-returns risk
Subject:  The first five years decide the next thirty
Preview:  Retire into a down market with no plan and the math turns against you.
Here's a risk that doesn't get enough attention: it's not just how your investments do over thirty years — it's when the bad years happen. A market drop early in retirement, while you're drawing income, does far more damage than the same drop later.
Withdraw from a shrinking balance and you lock in losses you never recover from. Two people with identical average returns can end up in completely different places based only on the order those returns arrived.
You can't control the market's timing. You can build a plan that doesn't force you to sell into a downturn — which is the whole point.
If you're within five years of retiring, this is the window to plan for:  Schedule your Wealth Curve Conversation →

EMAIL 5     Day 8     ·     Tax-efficient withdrawal order
Subject:  The order you spend money in is a strategy
Preview:  Taxable, tax-deferred, tax-free — the sequence matters.
Most retirees have money in three kinds of buckets: taxable (brokerage, savings), tax-deferred (IRA, 401k), and tax-free (Roth). The order you pull from those buckets can change your lifetime tax bill meaningfully — and most people default to the least efficient order without realizing it.
Draining one bucket before touching the others is simple, but simple isn't the same as smart. The right sequence keeps you in lower brackets longer, manages what's taxable each year, and protects flexibility down the road.
It's one of the highest-leverage, lowest-cost moves in retirement planning — and it's entirely within your control.

EMAIL 6     Day 10     ·     Roth conversion window
Subject:  The quiet window most people waste
Preview:  The years between retiring and RMDs are where the tax planning lives.
There's a window in early retirement that quietly disappears: the years after your paycheck stops but before required distributions and, often, Social Security begin. Your income dips — and so, temporarily, does your tax bracket.
For many households, that's the best time to consider moving money from tax-deferred to tax-free through Roth conversions — paying tax now, at a known lower rate, to avoid a larger, less predictable bill later.
It isn't right for everyone, and the amount matters. But the window is real, it's short, and once RMDs start it closes. Most people don't notice it until it's gone.

EMAIL 7     Day 12     ·     RMDs & bracket management
Subject:  The tax bill you can see coming
Preview:  RMDs don't manage themselves.
Required minimum distributions are one of the few tax events you can see years in advance — which makes it strange how often they catch people off guard. Once they begin, the government sets a minimum you must withdraw and pay tax on, whether you need the money or not.
For someone with a large pre-tax balance, RMDs can push income up, drag more of your Social Security into taxable territory, and even raise your Medicare premiums — all at once.
The time to soften that is the decade before it starts, not the year it hits. Bracket management is a long game, played early.

EMAIL 8     Day 14     ·     Social Security timing
Subject:  One box you can't un-check
Preview:  Claim at the wrong time and you can't take it back.
Social Security looks like a simple decision and isn't. Claim early, claim at full retirement age, or wait — each path changes your monthly check, your spouse's survivor benefit, and how much of it gets taxed.
The decision is close to permanent, and it interacts with everything else: your withdrawal order, your Roth conversions, your tax brackets. Optimizing it in isolation misses the point — it has to fit the whole plan.
There's no universally right age to claim. There's a right age for your situation, and it's worth getting that one right.
Before you file, it's worth running the whole picture:  Schedule your Wealth Curve Conversation →

Subject:  What we actually do
Preview:  Reduce taxes. Reduce fees. Turn savings into income. Add protection. Pass more on.
By now you've seen a pattern: none of these gaps live alone. Your withdrawal order affects your taxes, which affect your Medicare, which affects your income, which affects your legacy. Pull one thread and the others move.
That's the whole idea behind the way we work. We look at every facet — taxes, fees, income, protection, estate — and design one plan where the pieces work together instead of against each other.
