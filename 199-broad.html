Our Home on Broad Street

We could have planned from anywhere. We chose to put down roots in the middle of Red Bank — and to bring a building on Broad Street back to life while we did it.

Why we stayed

Built for the long term. Like the plans we write.

When John Smallwood acquired 199 Broad Street in November 2013, plenty of people told him to level it and build new. He saw a building worth keeping, and set out to turn a historic Red Bank home into a modern office for the firm, then based in Shrewsbury, without losing its charm.

George Sutton built the house in 1904. Its woodwork, stained glass, and detailed trim suggest it was meant as a showpiece. It stayed with the Sutton family into the 1950s, later became a boarding house, and was owned for a few years by Daniel O’Hern, who went on to serve on the New Jersey Supreme Court. From 1972 it was home to Bernard Hoffman’s law firm for roughly 40 years.

By 2013 it was wrapped in 1980s aluminum siding with rot behind it, and the rooms were hidden under pine paneling and old carpet. The restoration brought each room back as close to the original as possible.

In 2013 the house was hidden under aluminum siding, pine paneling, and old carpet. The restoration took it back toward the 1904 original.

The façade — 1980s aluminum siding came off; the front clapboard and about 80% of the original porch columns were saved.
Where clients arrive — original hardwood floors were found under decades of rugs.
